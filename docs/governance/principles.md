# Community Governance Principles

These principles are proposed for co-design with each participating community. They are not a substitute for a signed agreement or applicable law.

1. **Meaningful participation:** affected communities help define needs, acceptable uses and success measures.
2. **Local stewardship:** roles for ownership, administration, maintenance and decision-making are explicit.
3. **Data minimisation:** collect only information necessary for an agreed purpose.
4. **Transparency:** explain what the system does, its limitations, what data it processes and whether any external service is used.
5. **Human accountability:** people—not automated outputs—remain accountable for educational and other consequential decisions.
6. **Equitable access:** accessibility, language, disability inclusion and fair access are considered in design.
7. **Right to challenge:** users and guardians have a clear route to question, correct or report harmful outputs or misuse.
8. **Sustainable maintenance:** budgets, skills, spare parts, updates, backups and eventual retirement are planned.
9. **Open and portable:** avoid unnecessary lock-in; document migration, export and replacement options.
10. **Responsible exit:** communities can retrieve their data and safely decommission the system.

These principles must be adapted through local consultation and legal review before a pilot.
